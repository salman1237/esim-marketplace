=======================================
MERCHANT REGISTRATION PROCESS — TRIPTEL
=======================================


Version 1.0 · 28 September 2026

How a travel agency becomes an approved Triptel reseller, what it earns, and
what staff do at each step. Screenshots are from a local demo instance.

---


1. Who this is for
------------------

Travel agencies, tour operators, ticketing counters and online travel
platforms that want to sell eSIMs to their own customers and earn a
commission on each sale. A merchant can sell from a web portal, or integrate
Triptel's Partner API into their own system.

What a merchant gets

- A partner dashboard with sales, earnings and statements
- The ability to sell an eSIM to a walk-in traveller — including creating the
  traveller's account for them
- A commission percentage on every delivered sale, credited automatically
- API keys and webhooks for their own system
- Monthly statements with CSV export

What a merchant needs

- A registered business name
- A trade licence or corporate ID
- A business contact phone number
- A Triptel account (any ordinary customer account will do)

---


2. The process at a glance
--------------------------

    1. Sign up as a customer            (self-service)
            ↓
    2. Submit the B2B application       (self-service)
            ↓  emails → applicant, agent if quoted, admin, system@
    3. Admin reviews the application    (staff)
            ↓
    4. Admin sets the commission rate and, if applicable, the agent
            ↓
    5. Admin approves                   (staff)
            ↓  email → merchant, system@
    6. Merchant Portal appears; selling can begin
            ↓
    7. (optional) Complete KYC + add a payout account to withdraw earnings

Typical elapsed time is bounded by step 3, which is a human decision. Every
other step is immediate.

3. Step by step
---------------


Step 1 — Create an ordinary account

The applicant registers like any customer, with email and password or with
Google.

[screenshot: screenshots/07-register.png — Registration]

There is no separate "business sign-up". A merchant profile is attached to an
existing account, which is why an agency can browse and buy as a customer
before applying, and why an agent can also be a merchant.


Step 2 — Open the B2B application

Two routes lead to the same form:

| Route | When it is used |
|---|---|
| For Business in the header, or Become a partner in the footer banner | The agency found Triptel themselves |
| An agent's invite link, https://triptel.co/reseller?agent=<CODE> | An agent recruited them |

Applying directly:

[screenshot: screenshots/51-merchant-application-form.png — B2B application form]

Arriving via an agent's invite link — note that the **Agent Code field is
already filled in**:

[screenshot: screenshots/52-merchant-application-agent-code.png — B2B application with agent code pre-filled]


Step 3 — Fill in and submit

| Field | Required | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Registered Business Name | Yes | As it appears on the trade licence |
| Trade License / Corporate ID | Yes | Used by staff to verify the business exists |
| Business Contact Phone | Yes | How staff reach the agency about the application |
| Agent Code | No | Pre-filled when arriving from an invite link. Credits the agent who brought them in — it does not change the merchant's own commission |

Press Submit B2B Application. The account immediately shows a pending
state and the portal stays locked until a decision is made:

[screenshot: screenshots/54-merchant-application-pending.png — Application pending]

Emails sent automatically at this point:

| To | Content |
|---|---|
| The applicant | Confirmation that the application was received |
| The agent, if a code was quoted | Your agent code was used by <business> |
| Admin | A new B2B application is waiting |
| system@triptel.co | A copy, for the operational record |


Step 4 — Staff review

An admin opens Admin Console → Merchants. The tab label carries the count
of pending applications, so nothing sits unnoticed.

[screenshot: screenshots/31-admin-merchants.png — Merchant approval queue]

The reviewer sees the business name, trade licence, contact phone, the
applicant's account and the agent (if any). Before approving they:

1. Set the commission rate. The default is 5%. It is set per merchant, so
   negotiated terms are honoured.
2. Confirm or change the agent mapping. An admin can attach, change or
   remove the agent at any time, not only at approval.
3. Check the margin. If an agent is attached, the margin check shows
   whether the combined merchant rate plus agent rate still leaves the
   platform a margin at the current selling-profit percentage.

They then press Approve — or Reject with a reason, which is emailed to
the applicant.

> Approval is required from staff. Nothing an applicant types changes
> their commission or their status by itself.


Step 5 — Approved

Emails sent automatically:

| To | Content |
|---|---|
| The merchant | Approved, with their commission rate |
| system@triptel.co | A copy |

The Merchant Portal now appears in the header and the agency can start
selling immediately.

[screenshot: screenshots/19-merchant-overview.png — Merchant portal]

4. What the merchant does next
------------------------------


Sell an eSIM

Buy for customer — choose a destination and a package, enter the
traveller's email or username. If they have no account one is created for
them, and the eSIM plus QR code is emailed to them directly.

[screenshot: screenshots/20-merchant-provision.png — Sell to a customer]

The sale is paid from the merchant's wallet, so the merchant tops up in
advance (bKash, Nagad, bank transfer, or card via ShurjoPay) and draws down
as they sell.


Track orders

[screenshot: screenshots/21-merchant-orders.png — Merchant orders]


Collect commission

Commission is credited to the merchant's commission wallet **when the eSIM is
delivered** — never on a refunded order — and is calculated on the sale price
after any discount.

[screenshot: screenshots/22-merchant-commission.png — Commission]

To use or withdraw it, press the transfer button to move it into the main
balance.


Reconcile

[screenshot: screenshots/23-merchant-statement.png — Statements]

Monthly statements on screen and as CSV.


Integrate

[screenshot: screenshots/24-merchant-api-access.png — API access]

Issue an API key, set a webhook URL, and test the webhook from the same
screen. The API is documented at /api-docs and in
PARTNER_API_DOCUMENTATION.md (../PARTNER_API_DOCUMENTATION.md). Orders are
idempotent on the partner's own customer_reference, so a retry cannot
double-charge.

5. Getting paid out
-------------------

Commission is money in the platform. To take it out:

1. Move commission → main balance from the Commission tab.
2. Complete KYC — submit NID and document photographs, and wait for
   approval.
3. Save a payout account — a bank account or a mobile wallet such as
   bKash or Nagad.
4. Request a withdrawal. The amount is held from the main balance
   immediately.
5. Staff pay it, upload proof, and the conversion rate is frozen at that
   moment. Both sides get an email.

[screenshot: screenshots/13-customer-withdraw.png — Withdrawal request]

6. Commission explained
-----------------------

| Component | Who sets it | Default | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Merchant rate | Admin, per merchant | 5% | Sale price after discount |
| Volume tiers | Admin, platform-wide | none | If configured, cumulative delivered volume can override the flat rate |
| Agent bonus | Agent requests, admin approves | 0% | Deducted from the merchant's rate |

What the merchant sees is always the net rate. If a merchant is on 20% and
an agent has an approved 5% bonus, the merchant's dashboard, statement, ledger
line and API response all report 15% — the arrangement between Triptel and
the agent is not disclosed to the merchant. This is a deliberate business
rule.

Two protections apply:

- A bonus can never push a merchant's commission below zero; it is capped at
  the merchant's own rate.
- If the agent is suspended, no deduction is taken at all and the merchant
  receives their full rate.

7. Merchant states
------------------

| State | What it means | Who changes it |
|---|---|---|
| PENDING | Application submitted, awaiting review. Portal locked | — |
| APPROVED | Selling enabled, commission accruing | Admin |
| REJECTED | Declined with a reason, emailed to the applicant | Admin |
| SUSPENDED | Selling disabled; existing eSIMs unaffected | Admin |

---


8. Staff checklist
------------------

For the admin handling an application:

- [ ] Business name and trade licence look genuine and consistent
- [ ] Contact phone reachable
- [ ] Commission rate set deliberately — not left at the default by accident
- [ ] Agent mapping correct (present if they came via an invite link, absent
      if not)
- [ ] Margin check run if an agent is attached — merchant rate + agent rate
      must still leave the platform a margin
- [ ] Approve, or reject with a reason the applicant can act on

Every one of these actions is recorded in the audit log with actor, IP and
before/after state.

9. Frequently asked
-------------------

Can a merchant also be an agent?
Yes. The two are separate profiles on the same account; holding one does not
affect the other.

Can an agency apply without an agent code?
Yes — the field is optional. An agent can also be attached later by an admin,
or an agent can add a merchant who has no agent from their own portal.

Does quoting an agent code reduce the merchant's commission?
Not by itself. The agent's own 2% is paid on top of the merchant's rate.
Only a separately approved bonus reduces the merchant's rate, and the
merchant sees only the resulting net figure.

What happens if the eSIM fails to be delivered?
The order is marked FAILED, the merchant's wallet is refunded automatically,
and no commission is paid to anyone.

Can a merchant sell to someone who has no Triptel account?
Yes — the account is created as part of the sale and the eSIM is emailed to
the traveller.
